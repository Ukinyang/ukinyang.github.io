The problem

Coastal ecosystems are changing faster than our data can keep up

Tropical lagoons like Lagos and Badagry support millions of livelihoods, yet the baseline data needed to protect them is often thin, scattered across institutions, or stored in formats nobody downstream can use. Temperate fisheries face their own pressure from shifting stocks and warming water.

Fragmented monitoring

Zooplankton, benthic and water-quality data are collected in isolated projects that rarely connect with each other.

The overlooked middle step

Between a net tow and a policy brief lie months of microscopy, cleaning and statistics. This work is easy to underfund and easy to skip.

Fieldwork on tight resources

Labs in the Global South often work with fewer instruments and smaller budgets, so every result has to be earned with care and improvisation.

Studies I have carried out

MSc thesis, 2025University of BremenLagos Lagoon, Nigeria

Mesozooplankton community of the Lagos Lagoon, south-west Nigeria

I tested how zooplankton communities change along the lagoon, from river-influenced stations through the mid-lagoon to the ocean-facing coast, and across the shift from wet to dry season. I sampled 10 stations each month from October to December 2024 with a 55 µm plankton net, then sub-sampled with a Motoda splitter and identified and counted every sample under the stereomicroscope.

36taxa from 9 phyla
2.3Shannon diversity (H′)
0.63evenness (J′)
29samples from 10 stations

Cyclopoid copepods dominated in the wet season and calanoid copepods in the dry season, as rising salinity turned a freshwater community into a marine one. Abundance peaked in the mid-lagoon every month. Besides salinity, temperature, phosphate, chemical oxygen demand and trace metals emerged as key drivers, which points to growing human pressure on the lagoon. I ran the analysis in R with the vegan package, using Bray-Curtis cluster analysis and NMDS ordination with fitted environmental variables.

Supervisors: PD Dr. Barbara Niehoff (Alfred Wegener Institute) and Prof. Ikenna Charles Onyema (University of Lagos). A journal manuscript is in preparation. See the full analysis.

MSc thesis, 2019 to 2021University of LagosLagos and Badagry lagoons

Bacterial communities and water quality in the Lagos and Badagry lagoons

I investigated how bacterial communities relate to physicochemical water quality at five stations in the Lagos Lagoon and two in the Badagry Lagoon. I measured water parameters including dissolved oxygen and biochemical oxygen demand, isolated bacteria on selective media, counted colonies and confirmed isolates with biochemical tests. Selected isolates were identified by 16S rDNA sequencing at an external laboratory, and I analysed the resulting data myself. I also coordinated sampling days, procurement of materials and laboratory schedules for our student team.

270 × 107CFU/mL, Lagos Lagoon
210 × 107CFU/mL, Badagry Lagoon
7sampling stations

Sequencing matched the isolates to Escherichia coli, Enterobacter aerogenes, Bacillus subtilis, B. amyloliquefaciens, B. cereus and Brevibacillus agri. Both lagoons showed high levels of bacteria that are pathogenic to fish and people, so their water is unsafe for domestic use without treatment.

Data analysis

Inside the analysis: Lagos Lagoon mesozooplankton, 2024

I analysed 29 plankton samples alongside water quality data from the same stations and months to find out how the lagoon's zooplankton community changes and what shapes it. All figures below are from my MSc thesis and were made in R.

Workflow R code Study design Abundance Diversity Clustering Correlation Ordination

Workflow

  1. Sample10 stations along the river, mid-lagoon and coastal regions, monthly from October to December 2024. Horizontal hauls with a 55 µm plankton net (30 cm mouth), preserved in 4% formalin. Surface water analysed for physical and chemical parameters, nutrients, trace metals and chlorophyll pigments.
  2. Count and identifySub-sampling with a Motoda splitter, counting on a Bogorov plate and identification under a stereomicroscope at 20 to 40× magnification.
  3. StandardiseAbundance (ind. m−3) = organisms counted × splitting ratio ÷ volume of water filtered.
  4. DescribeShannon-Wiener diversity (H′) and Pielou's evenness (J′) for every station and month.
  5. GroupHierarchical clustering of stations: Bray-Curtis dissimilarity on log-transformed abundance for the community, Euclidean distance with Ward.D2 on scaled variables for the environment.
  6. RelatePearson correlation among variables, then NMDS ordination on Hellinger-transformed abundance with environmental variables fitted by envfit (999 permutations).
